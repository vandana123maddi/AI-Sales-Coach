# Domain research and prompt design

## 1. Buyer conversations

Real-estate buyer conversations are highly structured and usually move from broad interest to qualified intent. The best coaching should recognize whether a caller is a serious buyer or a casual enquirer and then ask the next question that materially advances the conversation.

| Topic | Typical buyer signal/question | What it means | Recommended agent response |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Property basics | "What type of property are you looking for?" | The buyer is still exploring fit. | Ask about property type, bed/bath needs, and non-negotiables. |
| Location | "We are looking around North London / near the station." | The buyer has a narrowed geography. | Confirm area, commute, school needs, or access requirements. |
| Budget | "We are looking around £600,000 to £700,000." | Budget is partly defined but must be confirmed. | Ask whether this is a hard ceiling or flexible range and whether financing is arranged. |
| Financing | "We have a mortgage in principle." | This is a serious buyer signal. | Acknowledge readiness and ask whether they want to view properties within the approved range. |
| Timing | "We need to move within 8–10 weeks." | Urgency is present. | Ask whether they are chain-free, have a buyer or seller, and whether they need to move quickly. |
| Viewing availability | "Are you available this weekend?" | The buyer is progressing toward action. | Offer two specific time options and confirm whether they want to arrange a viewing. |
| Competing properties | "We have seen two similar options already." | The buyer is comparing alternatives. | Ask which features matter most and what would make this property stand out. |
| Negotiation | "£20,000 below asking might be possible." | Buyer is discussing purchase strategy. | Clarify whether they want to test price flexibility or whether they need evidence on value. |
| Concerns | "The property needs work / the area feels noisy." | Objection is emerging. | Name the concern, ask one clarifying follow-up, and either offer a solution or suggest a comparison. |
| Serious buyer signal | "We are ready to view and can move quickly." | High intent and low uncertainty. | Offer a viewing, confirm financing or chain status, and propose a clear next step. |
| Casual enquirer | "We are just browsing and seeing what is out there." | Low urgency and low qualification. | Keep it lightweight and ask about broad criteria, timing, and whether they want updates. |

### Serious buyer vs casual enquirer

- Serious buyer: clear budget, location preference, timeframe, financing status, and viewing readiness.
- Casual enquirer: broad browsing, no deadline, no financing certainty, or no concrete property match.

The coach should treat these differently. A serious buyer should move toward viewings, availability, and offer readiness. A casual enquirer should stay exploratory and avoid asking for immediate commitment.

## 2. Vendor conversations

Vendor conversations typically centre on valuation, time on market, instruction, and urgency. The coaching should distinguish between a vendor casually testing the market and a vendor ready to appoint an agent.

| Concern | Signal | Interpretation | Recommended next move |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Property valuation | "What would you value it at?" | They want an evidence-based estimate. | Discuss valuation factors, comparables, and likely buyer demand before recommending a price. |
| Asking price | "We want £750,000 and it is already priced to sell." | Price expectation is firm. | Ask what motivates the target price and whether they have comparable evidence. |
| Marketing | "We want better exposure and more viewings." | The seller is focused on visibility. | Discuss marketing plan, target audience, and property positioning. |
| Time on market | "It has been on the market for 8 weeks." | There may be urgency or seller frustration. | Ask whether they want to re-price, relaunch, or change strategy. |
| Previous agent experience | "Our last agent did not bring enough buyers." | They are evaluating agent performance. | Ask what underperformed and what they want from a new strategy. |
| Urgency | "We need to move quickly due to a new job / sale chain." | There is pressure to act. | Clarify the move timeline and whether they need an urgent marketing plan. |
| Competing agents | "We have had three other agents call us." | They are comparing options. | Ask what differentiates what they want from an agent, not just price. |
| Offers | "We have a verbal offer but want to see if more comes." | There is active negotiation. | Clarify whether the offer is acceptable and what terms matter. |
| Instruction / mandate | "We want to list with you and sign a contract." | They are close to instruction. | Outline the marketing plan, fees, service level, and next appointment steps. |
| Seller expectations | "We need the highest price but quickly." | There is a tension between speed and value. | Reframe the decision around realistic market value, time-to-offer, and buyer traction. |

## 3. Sales progression

### 3.1 Buyer journey

#### Opening
- Objective: Start with a short, relevant opener and establish context.
- Signals: Buyer responds with interest, need, or a pause.
- Common objections: "We are just looking." "We are not ready yet." "We are not sure what we want."
- Good next move: Ask a single question about target property type, location, or urgency.
- Bad generic move: "Keep talking." or "Tell me more about your needs." without any structure.
- Trigger for the next stage: The buyer gives a concrete area, budget, or timing signal.

#### Discovery
- Objective: Understand the buyer's needs and must-haves.
- Signals: Specific area, type of property, number of bedrooms, family needs, commute.
- Common objections: "We are unsure about the area." "We are open-minded."
- Good next move: Narrow to likely property fits and ask about deal breakers.
- Bad generic move: Asking for a long life-story or listing every feature at once.
- Trigger for next stage: They reveal an actual property requirement or a shortlist.

#### Qualification
- Objective: Confirm budget, financing, and urgency.
- Signals: Mortgage in principle, offer readiness, budget range, timeline.
- Common objections: "We are still waiting on finance." "We need to speak with our partner."
- Good next move: Ask whether they are pre-approved and what is their highest realistic number.
- Bad generic move: Suggesting viewings before the financing range is clear.
- Trigger for next stage: Budget and timeline are roughly confirmed.

#### Property matching
- Objective: Match specific homes to these criteria.
- Signals: The buyer prefers a school area, price bracket, or commute.
- Common objections: "We saw similar homes but they were too expensive." "Not enough outdoor space."
- Good next move: Compare a few specific options and ask which features matter most.
- Bad generic move: Repeating generic property descriptions without a direct fit to their criteria.
- Trigger for next stage: The buyer is ready to view a shortlist.

#### Viewing
- Objective: Turn interest into action.
- Signals: Availability, willingness to meet schedule, interest in the property.
- Common objections: "We cannot make this week." "We need to think about it."
- Good next move: Offer two timeslots and ask which is most convenient.
- Bad generic move: Asking for another broad meeting without clear schedule options.
- Trigger for next stage: They agree, or explicitly say they are evaluating further.

#### Offer
- Objective: Clarify whether they are ready to move to offer terms.
- Signals: Real interest, discussion of price, and the buyer's risk appetite.
- Common objections: "We need to think about the valuation." "We need to know if there are further fees."
- Good next move: Confirm whether the deal is within their realistic ceiling and whether they need a final valuation discussion.
- Bad generic move: Telling them to "do what feels right" without a structured offer discussion.
- Trigger for next stage: They state a concrete offer intention or ask for a negotiation strategy.

#### Negotiation
- Objective: Shape the price and terms in a way that preserves trust and moves toward closing.
- Signals: Buyer cites price, survey, repair needs, or chain concerns.
- Common objections: "The property is too expensive." "We need a little flexibility on terms."
- Good next move: Acknowledge the concern and then ask whether the issue is price, condition, timing, or terms.
- Bad generic move: "It is a good property so I am sure it will work out." This is not grounded and does not clarify.
- Trigger for next stage: The buyer either agrees on price or asks for a revised offer structure.

#### Close
- Objective: Confirm the steps needed to proceed.
- Signals: Clear commitment, funding, legal/agent instructions, or final paperwork.
- Good next move: Recap the agreed terms and next action.
- Bad generic move: "Let me know if you think of anything else." without a timeline.
- Trigger for next stage: Formal instruction or a defined follow-up commitment.

### 3.2 Vendor journey

#### Opening
- Objective: Establish the seller's situation and the reason for the call.
- Signals: Motivation, timing, and impression of the market.
- Good next move: Ask what they want to achieve in the next 90 days.
- Bad generic move: Jumping straight to price without understanding seller goals.
- Trigger for next stage: Seller reveals the property, target timing, or reason for selling.

#### Property discovery
- Objective: Gather property facts, condition, and selling goals.
- Signals: Floor plan, condition, parking, garden, tenure, and buyer profile.
- Good next move: Ask what they believe makes the home attractive.
- Bad generic move: Repeating generic marketing points instead of matching to the actual property.
- Trigger for next stage: Seller provides enough facts for valuation and marketing discussion.

#### Valuation discussion
- Objective: Establish realism around market value and evidence.
- Signals: Asking price, comps, time on market, local demand.
- Common objections: "We know it is worth more." "We do not want to underprice it."
- Good next move: Discuss evidence, comparable sales, and likely buyer appetite.
- Bad generic move: Claiming a value without evidence.
- Trigger for next stage: A price range or selling strategy is established.

#### Motivation
- Objective: Understand urgency and chain pressure.
- Signals: Need to move quickly, new job, other purchase, probate, divorce, or relocation.
- Good next move: Clarify the deadline and the consequences of delay.
- Bad generic move: Ignoring urgency and speaking only about marketing.
- Trigger for next stage: The vendor's urgency becomes clear enough to guide action.

#### Pricing
- Objective: Align expectations with market reality.
- Signals: Seller's desired price vs realistic range.
- Common objections: "We do not want to reduce the price too soon." "We need a premium after refurbishment."
- Good next move: Review evidence for price, likely buyer pool, and negotiation room.
- Bad generic move: Telling them to list at any price without evidence.
- Trigger for next stage: They accept a pricing framework or ask for a listing recommendation.

#### Marketing discussion
- Objective: Explain visibility, buyer quality, and sales strategy.
- Signals: Need for exposure, agent differentiation, previous issues.
- Good next move: Outline channels, target buyer profile, and a lead-generation strategy.
- Bad generic move: Saying "We market it everywhere" without specifics.
- Trigger for next stage: They are ready to compare service and fees.

#### Instruction
- Objective: Turn interest into a formal listing relationship.
- Signals: They are ready to appoint, ask about contract, or negotiate fees.
- Good next move: Explain commission, marketing support, and next steps clearly.
- Bad generic move: Getting stuck in fee-only discussion without clarifying value for the seller.
- Trigger for next stage: They accept or reject instruction.

#### Close
- Objective: Confirm the appointment, plan, and follow-up.
- Signals: Agreement on listing, time to market, and communication cadence.
- Good next move: Summarize the property plan, marketing steps, and next review date.
- Bad generic move: Keep talking with no action or deadline.
- Trigger for next stage: Appointment, marketing launch, or follow-up call.

## 4. Sales signals

| Signal | Meaning | Effect on next-best move |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Budget confirmed | The buyer has a clear spending ceiling. | Move to property fit and viewing options. |
| Timeline confirmed | A move date or urgency is known. | Prioritize action and ask about availability or chain readiness. |
| Location preference | Clear area or travel constraints. | Narrow selection and rank common property fits. |
| Property preference | A style, school, layout, or amenity is known. | Discuss whether a specific listing meets the need. |
| Financing status | Mortgage approval or deposit status is known. | Decide whether to move toward viewing or resolve financing blockers. |
| Urgency | The buyer/vendor needs speed. | Use faster actions and fewer exploratory questions. |
| Viewing intent | The buyer wants to inspect a property. | Offer time slots and next steps. |
| Offer intent | The buyer is talking about making an offer. | Discuss conditions, price, or negotiation terms. |
| Objection | A concern is being raised. | Name it explicitly and ask a precise clarifying question. |
| Hesitation | The customer is not fully committed. | Use a lighter follow-up and confirm what would reduce uncertainty. |
| Price sensitivity | Price is an active barrier. | Address value, alternatives, or terms rather than generic reassurance. |
| Seller motivation | The vendor has a reason to move fast. | Make the sales plan time-sensitive and practical. |
| Readiness to instruct | Vendor is ready to appoint an agent. | Move to the agreement, marketing, and next steps. |

## 5. Prompt design

The actual application sends a concise, JSON-only sales coach payload to an OpenAI-compatible chat-completions endpoint. In the current implementation the fields are:

```json
{
  "observation": "...",
  "recommendedNextQuestion": "...",
  "currentConversationStage": "discovery",
  "buyerSentiment": "warm",
  "nextBestMove": "...",
  "objectionDetected": "...",
  "urgency": "medium",
  "evidenceTurnIds": ["turn-1", "turn-4"]
}
```

This matches the repository's real contract from `SalesCoachContext` and `CoachingResult` in the API server. The prompt therefore needs to ground all suggestions in the transcript and keep the output narrow enough to be useful at live-call speed.

### Prompt building blocks

The system should assemble its prompt from the following components:

- SYSTEM INSTRUCTIONS
- DOMAIN KNOWLEDGE
- CURRENT CALL STAGE
- BUYER/VENDOR TYPE
- ROLLING TRANSCRIPT
- RECENT TURNS
- KNOWN FACTS
- PREVIOUS COACH OUTPUT
- CURRENT OBJECTIVE

This is the same information flow the application already uses in practice: session ID, turn list, and chat-completion inference.

## 6. Prompt rules

The coach must:

1. Ground every suggestion in the transcript.
2. Never invent buyer or vendor facts.
3. Never give generic advice.
4. Prefer one specific next action.
5. Consider the current sales stage.
6. Consider buyer or vendor intent.
7. Consider objections.
8. Consider urgency.
9. Avoid repeating the previous suggestion.
10. Keep suggestions concise enough for live-call use.
11. Update as new transcript turns arrive.
12. Distinguish buyer and vendor speech.
13. Explain why the recommendation is appropriate.

## 7. Example coach outputs

### Example 1

Transcript:
"The property is nice, but the budget is fixed at £600,000. We cannot go higher."

Weak output:
"Address the price concern."

Strong output:
"The buyer has raised a clear budget objection. Acknowledge the concern and ask whether £600,000 is a hard ceiling or whether they could consider a lower price with terms or a different property mix."

Why the strong answer is better: it addresses the real objection, asks a clarifying question, and keeps the next move grounded in the buyer's stated constraint.

### Example 2

Transcript:
"We need to move in under six weeks because our tenancy ends on the 20th."

Strong output:
"The buyer has a clear urgency signal. Ask whether they are chain-free and offer a shortlist of properties that can be viewed quickly this week."

### Example 3

Transcript:
"We have a mortgage in principle and we can move quickly if we find the right place."

Strong output:
"The buyer is showing strong qualification and urgency. Move to viewing logistics: ask which times they are available and whether the area or school catchment is the top priority."

### Example 4

Transcript:
"We have had the property on the market for 11 weeks and the previous agent did not bring enough viewings."

Strong output:
"The vendor is signalling a performance problem and a potential re-marketing issue. Ask what the previous agent did not deliver and whether they want a fresh marketing strategy or a price review."

### Example 5

Transcript:
"The asking price feels too high given the size of the garden and the condition."

Strong output:
"The seller is raising a price objection. Ask what they see as the main value gap and whether they are open to a joint valuation review before deciding on the asking price."

### Example 6

Transcript:
"We are just browsing. We are not in a rush."

Strong output:
"The buyer is currently a casual enquirer rather than a serious prospect. Keep the conversation exploratory: ask what area or property type they are interested in and whether they want alerts when similar homes appear."

### Example 7

Transcript:
"We have not decided whether to reduce the price yet, but we are open to a realistic discussion."

Strong output:
"The vendor is not resistant but is price-sensitive. Recommend a valuation-based discussion and ask whether they want to understand likely buyer demand before making a pricing decision."

### Example 8

Transcript:
"We are considering making an offer if the survey is acceptable."

Strong output:
"This is an offer-intent signal. Ask what conditions they would be comfortable with, whether they need a survey and finance confirmation, and what price they would be prepared to test."

## 8. Live transcript handling

The live prompt should operate on a rolling transcript rather than a static snapshot. This is especially important for a call coach that reacts to new speech while the conversation is still happening.

- Rolling window: keep the most recent final turns and discard stale ones after a fixed limit.
- Recent turns: preserve only the latest meaningful final transcript segments and their speaker labels.
- Persistent facts: retain facts already established in the call, such as budget, timing, area preference, and financing status.
- Stage tracking: use a stage model that updates as the dialogue advances.
- Previous coach output: include the last recommendation so the model can avoid repeating itself.
- Avoiding hallucination: only summarize speaker data that has actually been said; if the transcript does not mention a fact, do not infer it.
- Update frequency: re-run the coach at a sensible cadence after every new final turn or after a short debounce window.
- Duplicate suppression: use stable transcript turn IDs and keep a dedupe set so repeated signals do not generate repeated coaching output.

## 9. Prompt design rationale

The assignment is evaluating whether the system demonstrates domain understanding in the prompt itself. This is why the coach is not generic: it uses real-estate-specific concepts such as budget ceilings, urgency, financing, property fit, time-on-market, pricing, marketing, and instruction. The prompt also distinguishes serious buyer and casual enquirer behaviour, which matters a lot in actual estate conversations.

The current implementation does this well because it forces the model to produce a grounded next move, cite evidence turn IDs, and avoid inventing facts. The strongest design choice is the requirement to base all guidance on transcript evidence. In a live-context sales call, that is the difference between helpful coaching and generic advice.
